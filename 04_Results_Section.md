## Associations with Depressive and Anxiety Symptoms

Pearson correlation analyses were conducted to examine the relationships between prospective cognition variables and affective symptoms.

Depression scores were significantly positively correlated with IFES Negative (r = .65, p < .001), intrusive visual imagery (r = .48, p < .001), intrusive verbal thought (r = .43, p < .001), and the perceived likelihood of negative future scenarios (r = .37, p < .001). In contrast, depression was negatively correlated with the likelihood of positive future scenarios (r = -.44, p < .001), vividness of positive future scenarios (r = -.29, p = .001), and overall sensory imagery ability (PSIQ Total; r = -.30, p < .001). The association with negative future vividness was weaker but still significant (r = .18, p = .046). Interpretation bias (HIT Negative) was not significantly correlated with depression (r = .06, p = .57).

A similar pattern was observed for anxiety symptoms. IFES Negative showed the strongest association (r = .52, p < .001), followed by intrusive verbal thought (r = .44, p < .001) and intrusive visual imagery (r = .37, p < .001). Positive future likelihood and sensory imagery ability were negatively associated with anxiety.

---

## Hierarchical Regression Predicting Depression

A three-step hierarchical regression was performed to examine the contribution of sensory imagery ability and prospective cognition variables to depressive symptoms, after controlling for age (N = 117).

In Step 1, age alone did not significantly predict depression (R² = .01, p = .31).  
In Step 2, adding PSIQ Total significantly improved the model (ΔR² = .09), with higher sensory imagery ability associated with lower depression scores.  
In Step 3, the inclusion of PIT positive vividness, PIT negative vividness, and IFES Negative led to a substantial increase in explained variance (ΔR² = .39), resulting in a final model that accounted for 49% of the variance in depression scores (Adj. R² = .47).

In the final model, IFES Negative emerged as the strongest predictor (β positive, p < .001). Higher vividness of negative future scenarios and lower vividness of positive future scenarios also significantly predicted greater depressive symptoms. PSIQ Total remained a significant negative predictor.

---

## Group Comparisons

Independent samples t-tests (Welch’s correction) were used to compare participants classified as depressed versus non-depressed.

Depressed participants reported significantly higher levels of IFES Negative (d = 1.22, p < .001), intrusive visual imagery (d = 1.02, p < .001), and intrusive verbal thought (d = 0.96, p < .001). They also showed lower likelihood and vividness of positive future scenarios, higher likelihood of negative future scenarios, and lower overall sensory imagery ability (PSIQ Total: d = -0.69, p < .001). Differences in negative future vividness did not reach statistical significance.

---

## Moderation Analysis

Moderation analyses were conducted to test whether sensory imagery ability (PSIQ Total) moderated the relationship between negative prospective cognition and depression.

The interaction between IFES Negative and PSIQ Total was not significant (p = .25). However, a significant interaction emerged between PIT negative vividness and PSIQ Total (p = .025). This indicates that the strength of the association between vividness of negative future scenarios and depressive symptoms varied as a function of individuals’ general sensory imagery ability.

---

## Mediation Analysis

Bootstrap-style path analyses (with Sobel test) were used to examine whether negative interpretation bias (HIT Negative) mediated the relationship between negative prospective cognition and depression.

HIT Negative did not significantly mediate the relationship between IFES Negative and depression (indirect effect ≈ -0.006, Sobel p = .57). Similarly, no significant mediation was found for the path from negative future likelihood through HIT Negative to depression (Sobel p = .75). Note that these analyses were based on a reduced sample (n ≈ 87) due to missing data on the HIT measure.

---

## Summary of Key Findings

1. Intrusive prospective imagery (IFES) was the most robust correlate and predictor of depressive symptoms.
2. A clear cognitive bias pattern was observed: reduced positive future imagery and elevated negative future imagery were associated with higher depression.
3. Lower sensory imagery ability was related to greater depressive symptoms and moderated the impact of negative future vividness.
4. Negative interpretation bias did not serve as a significant mediator in this sample.
