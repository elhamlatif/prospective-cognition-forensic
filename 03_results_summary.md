# Results Summary

**Project:** Quantitative Analysis of Prospective Cognition, Intrusive Imagery, and Affective Symptoms in a Forensic Sample  
**Author:** Elham Latif

---

## 1. Correlations

### With Depression Score
- IFES_Negative: r = 0.65, p < .001
- Intrusive_Visual: r = 0.48, p < .001
- Intrusive_Verbal: r = 0.43, p < .001
- PIT_Pos_Likelihood: r = -0.44, p < .001
- PIT_Neg_Likelihood: r = 0.37, p < .001
- PSIQ_Total: r = -0.30, p < .001
- PIT_Pos_Vivid: r = -0.29, p = .001
- PIT_Neg_Vivid: r = 0.18, p = .046
- HIT_Negative: r = 0.06, ns

### With Anxiety Score
- IFES_Negative: r = 0.52, p < .001
- Intrusive_Verbal: r = 0.44, p < .001
- Intrusive_Visual: r = 0.37, p < .001
- PIT_Pos_Likelihood: r = -0.33, p < .001
- PSIQ_Total: r = -0.29, p = .001

---

## 2. Hierarchical Regression (Predicting Depression)

- Step 1 (Age): R² ≈ 0.01
- Step 2 (+ PSIQ_Total): R² = 0.10
- Step 3 (+ PIT_Pos_Vivid, PIT_Neg_Vivid, IFES_Negative): **R² = 0.49**

Significant predictors in the final model:
- IFES_Negative (positive)
- PIT_Neg_Vivid (positive)
- PIT_Pos_Vivid (negative)
- PSIQ_Total (negative)

---

## 3. Group Differences (Depressed vs Non-depressed)

Depressed participants showed:
- Significantly higher IFES_Negative (d = 1.22)
- Higher intrusive visual and verbal cognition (d ≈ 1.0)
- Lower vividness and likelihood of positive future scenarios
- Higher likelihood of negative future scenarios
- Lower overall sensory imagery ability (PSIQ_Total)

---

## 4. Moderation

- PSIQ_Total did **not** significantly moderate the relationship between IFES_Negative and depression.
- PSIQ_Total **did** significantly moderate the relationship between PIT_Neg_Vivid and depression (interaction p = .025).

---

## 5. Mediation (Interpretation Bias)

- HIT_Negative did **not** significantly mediate the relationship between IFES_Negative and depression.
- HIT_Negative did **not** significantly mediate the relationship between PIT_Neg_Likelihood and depression.

Note: The sample size for analyses involving HIT was smaller (n ≈ 87) due to missing data.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Intrusive future-related imagery (IFES) is the strongest correlate and predictor of depressive symptoms in this forensic sample.
2. A clear bias pattern exists: reduced positive prospective imagery and elevated negative prospective imagery are associated with higher depression.
3. Sensory imagery ability is negatively related to depression and moderates the impact of negative future vividness.
4. Interpretation bias (HIT) did not show a clear mediating role in this dataset.
